Study information

Title

WP2-WP4 connection protocol: definition of risk group at stage of pregnancy

Description

The work in DP-Next intervention-arm (WP4) will be focused on reduction of diabetes risk in women who are pregnant and their families. The rationale for this is that perhaps the current definition of gestational diabetes in Denmark uses too high a threshold and that many women below the current GDM threshold are still at elevated risk of GDM at a subsequent pregnancy and at elevated risk of T2D later in life. Furthermore, there is a general impression that pregnancies are times when women and their families are more receptive to health messages. As there is already a coordinated network of care around pregnancies, it may be easier to identify a core set of stakeholders and work with them to map and remove barriers to healthy lifestyle offerings.

Objectives and hypotheses

Definition of risk group for intervention work package (WP4) in prediction work package (WP2)

DP-Next prediction-arm aims to develop diabetes risk prediction models for the entire population based exclusively on register-based data. This subproject for prediction group (WP2) will focus on calculating diabetes risk specifically for women in pregnancy, in order to provide a clear selection of at-risk women that intervention group (WP4) can focus on. An added benefit of focus on this specific group is that various additional relevant variables are available for pregnant women based on the registration of their first trimester pregnancy checkup. In particular BMI, smoking status and blood pressure are recorded, as well as HbA1c in many cases.

Specific aim:

  • Develop and validate a prediction model that identifies women at high risk of type 2 diabetes after the time of giving birth

Design plan for derivation of specific pregnancy-connected risk prediction model

Study population (model derivation)

WP2 will derive the model for a cohort of women who:

  • Have been pregnant at least once between 2010 and 2015

  • Do not have a previous diagnosis of type 1 or type 2 diabetes

Time window for prediction

The model will predict the 10-year risk of registry-defined incident T2D. We chose a 10-year horizon because the population is young and few will develop T2D within a shorter period. If HbA1c data allow, secondary outcomes will be HbA1c levels at 5 and 10 years after the index birth and HbA1c trajectories over this period.

Definition of available data for prediction

The prediction models will be built based on general register-based data supplemented with the results of the women’s first, third trimester and post-partum checkups. As the aim is to focus the intervention on the post-partum period, risk indicators from pre-pregnancy and all pregnancy stages as well as delivery can be incorporated in the risk model.

  • Pre-pregnancy: based on pre-existing register-based data, following the same general-population strategy defined in WP2.

  • First trimester: additional data collected at the first trimester pregnancy checkup

  • Third trimester: additional data collected at subsequent pregnancy checkups (We need to check availability)

  • Post-partum: additional data collected around the delivery, early health status of the baby and post-partum checkups:

Statistical methods

Descriptive statistics

First, we will characterise the study population by demographic factors (age, ethnicity, socioeconomic position) and by pregnancy related factors from the first trimester and the postpartum period. Using the OSDC algorithm, we will examine trends in the incidence of type 2 diabetes in the population and across regions.

Variable processing

We will include predefined variables based on their established association with the risk of type 2 diabetes, as listed in Definition of the potential predicters for prediction. Diagnoses and medications may carry hidden indications for type 2 diabetes risk; however, the number of potential indicators is large. To select candidates for our prediction model, we will create a play dataset based on a pregnancy population from 2004 to 2010 to define the set of predictors. Women with pregnancies in this period and after 2010 are excluded from play data, as their potential event must only be in the development data. We will include a 10 year history of each diagnosis and medication and uniformly test the association between each diagnosis and each medication and 10 year type 2 diabetes risk. We will produce a Manhattan plot with groupings of ICD-10 and ATC codes on the x axis. We will bring the candidates to domain experts in pregnancy related metabolic factors from SDCA and SDCC and discuss: (1) whether the indications are biased; and (2) the opportunity to collapse overlapping predictors (for example, a diagnosis of hypertension together with a prescription for antihypertensive medication). In a second step, we will use the combined variables and estimate each one 10 year risk with diabetes . We will then produce a plot with prevalence in the population on the x axis and the magnitude of the association on the y axis. In a second iteration with the domain experts, we will discuss the relevance of each predictor based on prevalence of the diagnosis and magnitude of risk. The candidates remaining after this discussion will be included as candidate predictors for the main model.

Statistical model selection

With all predictors defined in the play data, we will continue to use these data to select our candidate statistical models. The dataset will be provided to the team of prediction modellers, who will compete to create the best performing model while remaining methodologically available to each other. As the primary outcome, the model will aim to predict events of type 2 diabetes and, if the data allow, to predict 5 year HbA1c levels after pregnancy. The methods available for the modellers to choose from could include Cox proportional hazards, Poisson regression, LASSO, and random forest, each of which could be applied separately or combined in a super learner. The candidate models selected on the basis of this step will then be brought to the development dataset.

Developing prediction model

Once all predictors and statistical models have been defined, we will use the training dataset from pregnant women in the period 2010 to 2013. A prior HbA1c before index is available for 10% of women in 2010, 25% in 2015 and 40% in 2020. We will handle prior HbA1c in three ways, 1) an indicator of a present or absent measurement togehter with calendar-time, 2) a submodel restricted to complete cases, and 3) multiple imputation from the markers available at baseline. All models will be included in a super learner which will choose the best performing model using cross validation based on the Brier score.

Validation

For validation, we will use two approaches: first, we will subset one region from the development dataset to test performance across regions; second, we will subset the two latest years, 2013 to 2015, to test performance on newer data, which will reflect the situation when the model is deployed on the 2027 population. Predictive performance will be assessed by the Brier score net benifit, and decision curve analysis. We wiil subdive and test performance across age-groups, parity, and HbA1c present or absent at baseline.

Choosing and creating the final prediction model

The best performing model from the super learner will be trained on data from the entire period from 2010 to 2015 and exported from the protected enviorment it was developed in. It will then be built into an R Shiny app, which will be provided to the group in WP4. Our model will provide an estimate of the 10 year risk of type 2 diabetes and the expected 5 year HbA1c after pregnancy. Our approach follows established principles in machine learning and statistical modeling, adhering to standardized practices for prediction, reporting, and validation [@collins2024; @lopez-ayala2025; @collins2024a; @riley2024].

Definition of focus group for WP4

After the pregnancy risk model has been developed, it can be run for more recent age cohorts, and an up-to-date descriptive analysis can be made of the distribution of long-term T2D risk among recently pregnant women. This descriptive analysis will show which data are most informative for the estimation of future diabetes risk. It can also be used to explore geographical variation and clustering of risk factors. WP4 can then choose a more focused group of high-risk women in the postpartum period, and geographical settings. It may be worth considering focusing on GPs that measure HbA1c routinely in pregnant women (improved risk prediction), and on women above a given BMI threshold.

Perspective

Our long-term goal is to implement the model as a clinically ready tool at the postpartum check-up in general practice, so that women at high risk of type 2 diabetes can be offered prevention early.
